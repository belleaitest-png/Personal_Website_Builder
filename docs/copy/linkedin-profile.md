## 2. About section (2,600 character limit)

```
I trained as an accountant. I have never held an engineering job. I have three products live and two more in development, and I built all of them with AI agents.

That gap is the most interesting thing about this moment, and it is the work I want next.

My background is audit and advisory: Deloitte, then AlixPartners on turnaround and restructuring, ICAEW qualified, now an MBA at Harvard Business School (Class of 2026). What audit actually teaches you is how to read a system, find the number that does not reconcile, and build the case for change. It turns out that is most of the job when you are pointing a model at a real problem. The model handles the syntax. Someone still has to know which problem is worth solving and describe it precisely enough to be useful.

So I started building.

Apex Health OS reads my WHOOP recovery, HRV, strain and sleep data, correlates it against what I eat, and generates the grocery list my biology asked for. Built with Claude Code.

Bloom is a trimester-aware pregnancy platform that surfaces the right supplements and flags nutrient gaps, so nobody has to Google at 2am. Built end to end in Lovable.

Demographic Forecaster maps country-level fertility decline and projects population trajectories. It started as a chart I needed for an HBS paper and turned into the thing that made the argument.

Two more are in development: an agent-based personal CRM, and a supplement manager that catches ingredient overlap across brands.

The domain is food, fertility and metabolic health, because that is where I have done the research and where the data is richest and least used. My HBS work on declining birth rates and food systems kept hitting the same wall: the evidence exists, spread across subsidy data, processing incentives and nutrition panels nobody reads together. That is no longer a research problem. It is an integration problem, and these tools are unreasonably good at integration.

Two things I believe:

The constraint was never who could code. The people who understand a problem deeply are becoming the people who fix it, and most organisations have not restructured for that yet.

The most important companies of the next decade will sit where biology, food and AI meet. Not because AI is the interesting part, but because biology now produces more data than anyone can read unaided.

I evaluated 30 AI tools and kept four. I write about all of it publicly, including what did not work.

Looking for applied AI work where the hard part is the domain, not the demo.

Portfolio and thesis: annabellebody.com
```
